= Shared responsibility model
:page-summary: How operational responsibilities are divided between Akka and the customer when running Akka Automated Operations (AAO) under the Dedicated, BYOC, and BYOK8s deployment models.
:page-persona: operator-platform, operator-sre
:page-aliases: operations:production-readiness/dedicated/shared-responsibility.adoc, operations:production-readiness/byoc/shared-responsibility.adoc, operations:production-readiness/byok8s/shared-responsibility.adoc

include::ROOT:partial$include.adoc[]

This page describes how responsibilities are divided between Akka and the customer when running Akka Automated Operations (AAO) under three deployment models:

* *Dedicated:* Akka owns and operates the cloud account and runs the platform within it.
* *BYOC (Bring Your Own Cloud):* AAO runs inside the customer's cloud account.
* *BYOK8s (Bring Your Own Kubernetes):* Akka runs inside a Kubernetes cluster the customer provides and operates.

See xref:operations:index.adoc[Choose how you run Akka] for how the models compare.

The at-a-glance summary and the per-area sections below assign an owner to each activity. Where the owner or the split differs by model, the *Applies to* column names the models a row covers, and the activity appears once for each variant; `All` means the row is the same in every model. For the authoritative row-by-row breakdown, with the full Responsible / Accountable / Consulted / Informed split, see the xref:operations:production-readiness/raci.adoc[RACI].

[NOTE]
====
The policies published on {url-akka-trust-center}[trust.akka.io] and the Customer Support Policy take precedence over any summary here.
====

== Executive summary

Akka Automated Operations is a managed platform. What it covers depends on the deployment model:

* *Dedicated:* Akka owns and operates the cloud account that hosts the platform and runs everything within: the runtime, operators, encryption, region installation, scaling, patching, platform observability, backups, the in-region network, and the security monitoring over the account. That leaves you the least to run of the three models: you own the applications you build with the Akka SDK, the data they process, any customer-managed encryption keys or TLS certificates you bring, and your side of the operational partnership.
* *BYOC:* the platform runs inside your cloud account. Akka takes responsibility for the platform itself: the runtime, operators, encryption, region installation, scaling, patching, observability of the platform, backups, and the operational rituals that surround them. You take responsibility for the cloud account, the network you bring, the human identities that access the Akka console, the applications you build with the Akka SDK, and the data those applications process. Because AAO operates inside your cloud account, the model is genuinely shared: Akka manages the platform layer end-to-end, and you manage the layers above and below it.
* *BYOK8s:* Akka runs inside a Kubernetes cluster you provide and operate. Akka takes responsibility for the platform itself: the runtime, operators, inter-component mTLS, region installation, scaling, patching, observability of the platform, and the operational rituals that surround them. Encryption of customer data (database at rest and in transit, ingress TLS) is your responsibility (see Section 6). You take responsibility for the cloud account, the Kubernetes cluster and its underlying infrastructure, database backups, the network, the human identities that access the Akka console, the applications built with the Akka SDK, and the data those applications process. Because Akka runs inside infrastructure you operate, the model is genuinely shared, and more activities are jointly owned than in the other models.

In every model, some activities are jointly owned, with the lead and consulted parties named explicitly.

[NOTE]
.*What you own in BYOK8s*
====
In BYOK8s you own the infrastructure beneath the platform, not just the application. Beyond the application, you own:

* The *Kubernetes cluster*: creation, node pools, capacity and scaling, patching, and version upgrades.
* The *database*: provisioning, capacity, version upgrades, backups, and restores.
* The *network and ingress*: connectivity, ingress TLS (provide a certificate issuer or grant Akka DNS access), and IP allowlists.
* The *Teleport proxy* the Federation Plane uses to reach the cluster: install, upgrade, and operational health.
* *Encryption of your data* at rest and in transit (database and ingress).
* *Cluster and node audit logs* (the Kubernetes and infrastructure layer you operate).
* For *Business Continuity* (multi-region): standing up and operating a cluster, database, and cross-region networking in each region.

Akka owns the platform layer: the runtime, operators, inter-component mTLS, region installation, scaling logic, platform observability, and releases.
====

Shared responsibility extends to going live. Akka has a set of standards it expects of customer applications before flipping prod traffic on: sizing, observability, CI/CD, functional and non-functional testing, DR (disaster recovery) and rollback rehearsal. The xref:operations:production-readiness/checklist.adoc[production readiness checklist] captures those standards and the sign-off gate Akka recommends before launch.

[IMPORTANT]
.*Catch-all*
====
Anything not explicitly listed in this document is the customer's responsibility. This includes customer application code, customer-side operational tooling, and any concern not explicitly assigned to Akka. The customer may request additional operational support from Akka through the standard support channel.
====

== Layered view of AAO

The customer owns the applications in every model. Beneath them:

* *Dedicated:* Akka owns and operates the cloud account and the platform layer beneath the applications.
* *BYOC:* the customer owns the underlying cloud account; Akka manages the platform layer in between.
* *BYOK8s:* the customer owns the underlying cloud account, Kubernetes cluster, and database; Akka runs inside that cluster and manages the platform components.

== How to read this

Each activity in the matrix has an owner, the party that is Responsible and Accountable. The other party may be Consulted or Informed; called out in the description where relevant.

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Owner | Meaning

| Customer
| The customer is Responsible and Accountable for the activity. Akka may be Consulted or Informed.

| Akka
| Akka is Responsible and Accountable for the activity. The customer may be Consulted or Informed.

| Shared
| Akka and the customer share responsibility. The split is described in the row's description.
|===

In Dedicated, because Akka owns and operates the cloud infrastructure, there is little customer-side infrastructure to coordinate on, though some aspects, such as sizing and capacity, are still worked through jointly with your Akka team, as is any network you choose to integrate with the Akka region (for example, a transit gateway). In BYOC and BYOK8s, because Akka operates inside infrastructure you control, coordinate with Akka before infrastructure changes that could affect the Region.

Reaching the reliability and availability your deployment is built for depends on both sides meeting the responsibilities below, worked through with your Akka team; your agreement and the Federation Plane SLA Policy remain the authoritative statement of what is committed. See the xref:operations:production-readiness/index.adoc[production readiness overview] and the xref:operations:production-readiness/checklist.adoc[production readiness checklist].

== 1. Cloud account

In Dedicated, Akka owns and operates the cloud-provider account that hosts the Akka region. Akka provisions the account, pays the associated bill, holds the account-level IAM, and operates everything inside it; the customer's cost is set by an agreed structure. The customer does not own or operate the cloud account.

In BYOC and BYOK8s, the customer owns the cloud-provider account that hosts the Akka region. Akka receives narrowly scoped programmatic access to install, operate, and maintain the platform inside that account. The customer can revoke that access at any time.

At a glance, in Dedicated::
* *Akka*: Provisions, owns, pays for, and operates the dedicated cloud account. Sets quotas and holds all account IAM.
* *Customer*: Is consulted on expected usage so Akka can size quotas appropriately. Does not own or operate the account.

At a glance, in BYOC and BYOK8s::
* *Customer*: Owns the cloud account, pays the bill, and creates the IAM permissions that Akka uses. Can cut off Akka's access at any time.
* *Akka*: In BYOC, documents the required permissions and provides the akka-bootstrap utility, and operates within the IAM scope the customer grants, and nothing outside it. In BYOK8s, documents the access Akka requires into the customer's cluster, and operates within the access the customer grants, and nothing outside it.

.*Responsibilities*

====

== 2. Networking and connectivity

Akka creates the VPC (Virtual Private Cloud) and all in-region networking using cloud-provider best practices.

In Dedicated, Akka does this within the account it owns. Because Akka owns the account, the surrounding network is Akka's to operate as well: CIDR allocation, peering, IP allowlists, and DNS. The customer is involved only where it integrates its own network with the Akka region, for example a transit gateway.

In BYOC and BYOK8s, the customer provides the CIDR range and owns the surrounding network: peering or hub-and-spoke topologies, IP allowlists, DNS, and any private-connectivity bridge to the corporate network.

At a glance, in Dedicated::
* *Akka*: Builds and operates the in-region VPC, load balancers, and certificate issuer, along with CIDR allocation, peering, IP allowlists, and DNS.
* *Customer*: Coordinates with Akka only where it integrates its own corporate network with the Akka region (for example, a transit gateway), and provides the address ranges Akka uses for allowlists.

At a glance, in BYOC and BYOK8s::
* *Akka*: Builds the in-region VPC, load balancers, and certificate issuer. Publishes the connectivity-pattern whitepaper so the customer can choose.
* *Customer*: Allocates the CIDR block, configures peering or hub-and-spoke to the corporate network, maintains IP allowlists and DNS records.

====

| Activity | Applies to | Owner | Description

| Private connectivity | Dedicated | Akka | Akka operates connectivity within its own VPC. Where the customer integrates its own network (for example, a transit gateway), the parties coordinate on the integration.
| Private connectivity | BYOK8s | Customer | The customer designs, implements, and operates private connectivity into and out of the cluster using whatever pattern fits their environment.
| Ingress/egress | Dedicated | Akka | Akka installs the load balancer and certificate issuer (Let's Encrypt by default) for the region's platform APIs and generated application hostnames. | VPC peering / hub-and-spoke | BYOC and BYOK8s | Customer | Customer configures peering or hub-and-spoke between the Akka region's VPC and other corporate networks. Availability depends on cloud provider.

| IP allowlist on NAT and load balancers | BYOC | Customer | Customer configures and maintains the IP allowlist for the NAT (Network Address Translation) gateways and the public load balancer.
| IP allowlist on NAT and load balancers | BYOK8s | Customer | Customer configures and maintains the IP allowlist for the NAT (Network Address Translation) gateways and the public load balancer. The customer informs Akka of changes that may affect Platform connectivity.
| DNS records | Dedicated | Akka | Akka creates and maintains DNS records for platform machinery and applications in the cloud-provider DNS zones for the installation. Customer is informed.
| DNS records | BYOC and BYOK8s | Customer | Customer creates and maintains DNS records for platform machinery and applications in the cloud-provider DNS zones configured by Akka.

== 3. Platform setup and bootstrap

In Dedicated and BYOC, Akka provisions the Akka region into the cloud account (the dedicated account Akka operates, or the customer's account in BYOC), federates it to the Akka Federation Plane, and runs smoke tests before handing it over. In BYOK8s, Akka installs the platform machinery into the Kubernetes cluster the customer has provisioned, federates it to the Akka Federation Plane, and runs smoke tests before handing it over.

At a glance, in Dedicated and BYOC::
* *Akka*: Provisions the region (networking, K8s, persistence, registry, observability, operators), installs Teleport for federation, and configures region groups.
* *Customer*: Identifies region groupings and provides a preferred maintenance window that Akka aims to use for operations that may cause downtime.

At a glance, in BYOK8s::
* *Akka*: Installs the platform machinery (Akka runtime, operators, observability) into the customer's cluster and configures region groups.
* *Customer*: Provisions the cluster and database, installs Teleport for federation using the short-lived token Akka provides, identifies region groupings, and provides a preferred maintenance window that Akka aims to use for operations that may cause downtime.

.*Responsibilities*

====

| Platform bootstrapping | BYOK8s | Akka | Akka installs the platform machinery (runtime, operators, observability) into the customer's Kubernetes cluster after the customer provisions the underlying infrastructure.

| Region group configuration | All | Akka | Akka configures region groups on the Federation Plane to match the customer's groupings.

== 4. Platform maintenance and releases

Akka manages the lifecycle of the platform itself: patching, runtime/infra releases, and security remediations. In Dedicated and BYOC, that includes Kubernetes and database upgrades. In BYOK8s, the customer operates Kubernetes and database upgrades on their own infrastructure, consulting Akka first on certified versions.

At a glance, in Dedicated and BYOC::
* *Akka*: Patches infrastructure, upgrades Kubernetes and the database, and ships regular releases of the runtime, region machinery, and Federation Plane.
* *Customer*: Receives advance notice of any maintenance that causes downtime; Akka aims to schedule such work within the preferred maintenance window.

At a glance, in BYOK8s::
* *Akka*: Patches platform infrastructure (JVM, container base image, Akka runtime) and ships regular releases of the runtime, region machinery, and Federation Plane.
* *Customer*: Schedules and runs K8s and database upgrades after consulting Akka on certified versions. Receives advance notice of Akka-led maintenance.

Vulnerability reports available from trust.akka.io.

== 5. Operations and scaling

Akka auto-scales compute and persistence within the region's defined sizing standards. In Dedicated, because Akka owns the cloud account, Akka also pays the associated bill and reviews the account's charges. In BYOC and BYOK8s, the customer retains visibility and control over cost and capacity decisions with business impact. In BYOK8s, Akka elastically scales the application workloads to match traffic, and this depends on the customer ensuring the underlying cluster and database have capacity headroom.

At a glance::
* *Akka*: Auto-provisions and de-provisions compute and persistence to match utilization. HA (high availability) is the default. Triages and prioritizes customer feature requests. In Dedicated, also pays the cloud bill and reviews the account's charges.
* *Customer*: Selects sizing options, and signals expected demand spikes (e.g., performance testing) so Akka can pre-warm. In BYOC and BYOK8s, also reviews their cloud-account spend.

| Cost controls | BYOC and BYOK8s | Customer | Customer reviews their cloud-account subscription and monthly charges. Akka auto-provisions and de-provisions to match utilization; HA is the default posture; sizing options keep cost in check.

== 6. Encryption, keys, and certificates

In Dedicated and BYOC, all data in the application plane is encrypted at rest and in transit. Customers can rely on Akka-managed encryption or bring their own keys for the database.

In BYOK8s, encryption of customer data is the customer's responsibility: database (at rest and in transit) and ingress TLS in transit (either by providing a certificate issuer or by granting Akka enough access to solve DNS-01 / HTTP-01 challenges). Akka manages encryption internal to the platform: mTLS between Akka components and TLS to the Federation Plane (see Appendix A), and encryption of secrets synced from the Federation Plane (see Appendix B).

In every model, the lifecycle of cryptographic materials follows Akka's Key Management and Cryptography Policy.

At a glance, in Dedicated and BYOC::
* *Akka*: Encrypts data in transit (TLS) and at rest using cloud-provider encryption services. Default keys are Akka-managed.
* *Customer*: Maintains key material in their KMS when CMEK is used; provides TLS certificates for private load balancers or customer-owned domains.

At a glance, in BYOK8s::
* *Customer*: Owns customer-data encryption end-to-end: database (at rest and in transit), ingress TLS via certificate issuer or DNS/HTTP-01 access. Manages CMEK key material.
* *Akka*: Manages encryption internal to the platform: mTLS between Akka components, TLS to the Federation Plane, and encryption of FP-synced secrets.

.*Responsibilities*
[%collapsible]
====
[cols="2,1,1,4", options="header"]

== 7. Observability and monitoring

Monitoring follows the same platform/application boundary as the rest of this model: Akka watches the platform layer it operates, and the customer watches their own applications. In Dedicated, because Akka owns the cloud account, Akka also handles the account's security monitoring. In BYOC, the customer also watches the access tooling on their side; in BYOK8s, the cluster and the access tooling on their side. Application telemetry flows to whichever observability vendor the customer chooses.

At a glance::
* *Akka*: Watches the health of the platform it operates, for example its system and infrastructure pods, database health and resource use, platform runtime errors, and internal processing-lag signals, and acts on them as part of running the region. Also tracks platform availability and region telemetry, with uptime reports on request. In Dedicated, also owns cloud-native audit logging, SIEM / security monitoring, and cloud security posture for the dedicated account.
* *Customer*: Watches their own Akka applications, for example pod resource use, projection health, and any service-specific metrics, and sets the thresholds that make sense for their services. Exports application telemetry over OTLP. In BYOC and BYOK8s, also configures Teleport alerting on their side and owns cloud-native audit logging on the cloud account. In BYOK8s, also watches the cluster, node, and database infrastructure they operate.

[NOTE]
====
This lays out the division of responsibility between the platform and application layers. It is not a fixed catalog of alerts, a set of committed thresholds, or a service-level guarantee, the signals and tooling Akka uses to run the platform change over time, and availability and support commitments live in the Akka Federation Plane SLA Policy and the Customer Support Policy. Monitoring of the customer's own application pods sits with the customer rather than Akka, since they are best placed to define what healthy looks like for their services, and Akka can share recommended metrics and thresholds on request.

== 8. Backups, disaster recovery, and multi-region

In Dedicated, Akka provides database point-in-time recovery, Kubernetes resource backups, and multi-region failover tooling, and replication topology is chosen with the customer. In BYOC, Akka provides database point-in-time recovery and multi-region failover tooling, and disaster-recovery exercises and replication topology are joint decisions. In BYOK8s, the customer runs the database with point-in-time recovery (PITR) on their infrastructure, and Akka uses Velero for Kubernetes resource backups with infrastructure the customer provides; restore testing, disaster-recovery exercises, and replication topology are joint decisions, and Akka provides multi-region failover tooling.

Multi-region replication is asynchronous. RTO/RPO targets are set by the agreement; see Appendix B and the xref:operations:production-readiness/disaster-recovery.adoc[disaster recovery scenario catalog] for what a regional failover can lose.

At a glance, in Dedicated::
* *Akka*: Provides database point-in-time recovery, Velero-based Kubernetes backups, periodic restore testing, and multi-region failover tooling and runbooks. RTO/RPO targets are set by the agreement.
* *Customer*: Specifies replication mode per service; is consulted before failover.

At a glance, in BYOC::
* *Akka*: Provides database point-in-time recovery, Velero K8s backups, periodic restore testing, and multi-region failover tooling and runbooks. RTO/RPO targets are set by the agreement.
* *Customer*: Notifies Akka after ad-hoc backups; specifies replication mode per service; is consulted before failover.
* *Shared*: Annual disaster-recovery exercise (or as agreed) jointly run by Akka and the customer.

At a glance, in BYOK8s::
* *Akka*: Operates Velero for K8s resource backups (against customer-provided IAM and object storage), provides multi-region failover tooling and runbooks. RTO/RPO targets are set by the agreement.
* *Customer*: Configures database point-in-time recovery (PITR), notifies Akka after ad-hoc backups, specifies replication mode per service, is consulted before failover.
* *Shared*: Velero (customer provides IAM/storage, Akka operates), restore testing, and the annual or as-agreed disaster-recovery exercise.

== Appendix A: Architecture in brief

.*Details*
[%collapsible]
====
AAO consists of two planes:

* *Federation Plane*, a globally-accessible coordination service managed by Akka. Handles organizations, accounts, role assignments, key rotation, access tokens, and application deployment across regions. Designed to be highly resilient. In the rare event that the Federation Plane is unavailable, applications running in the Application Plane continue to operate.
* *Application Plane*, one or more federated regions. In Dedicated, each region runs independently inside the dedicated cloud account that Akka owns and operates; in BYOC, inside the customer's cloud account. Each region contains a Kubernetes cluster, a persistence store, a container registry, and Akka operators for routing, elasticity, deployment, certificate management, key rotation, and cross-region failover. In BYOK8s, each region runs inside a Kubernetes cluster that the customer provides and operates inside their cloud account. The cluster hosts the Akka operators for routing, elasticity, deployment, certificate management, key rotation, and cross-region failover, together with the persistence store. The customer is responsible for the cluster, its upgrades, the database, and their own container registry; Akka manages the Akka components running on top.

Communication between Federation Plane and Application Plane uses TLS with token-based authentication. Inter-region communication between applications uses mutual TLS (mTLS) with per-component certificates.
====

== Appendix B: Data protection in brief

.*Details*
[%collapsible]
====
All application data stays within the cloud region and VPC of the installation (the dedicated cloud account in Dedicated, the customer's cloud account in BYOC and BYOK8s) and is isolated from the Federation Plane. Data at rest is encrypted using the cloud provider's encryption mechanism. Customers can rely on Akka-managed encryption or use Customer-Managed Encryption Keys (CMEK).

Akka applies an additional encryption layer for sensitive material that is synced from the Federation Plane to regional execution clusters: project secrets, authentication tokens, OpenID refresh tokens, and customer-supplied secrets. Lifecycle, rotation, and access control of cryptographic materials follow the Akka Key Management and Cryptography Policy.

[discrete]
=== Recovery objectives (RTO/RPO)

RTO and RPO targets are defined in the customer's agreement. Multi-region replication is asynchronous, so a regional failover can lose the most recent writes that had not yet replicated to the surviving region. The xref:operations:production-readiness/disaster-recovery.adoc[disaster recovery scenario catalog] describes each case.

Compliance posture (SOC 2 Type II, annual third-party penetration test, current subprocessor list) is published on {url-akka-trust-center}[trust.akka.io].
====

== Appendix C: Private connectivity via Teleport

.*Details*
[%collapsible]
====
Some enterprises require that Kubernetes API servers have no public endpoint. Akka supports this through Teleport, an identity-aware proxy and certificate authority that creates an authenticated, encrypted bridge between the Federation Plane and private Kubernetes clusters.

* No public Kubernetes API endpoints, API servers remain entirely private.
* Reverse tunnels, Teleport agents in each region initiate outbound connections to the Teleport proxy; the Federation Plane never connects directly into the region.
* No long-lived credentials in Dedicated and BYOC, where Akka installs Teleport: the Federation Plane authenticates through workload identity and receives short-lived certificates.
* Complete audit trail, all authentication and API calls are logged. Audit logs are available to the customer on request.
* Cloud-portable, works across AWS, GCP, Azure, and on-premises.
====
